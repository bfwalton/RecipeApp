Most recipe apps stop caring the moment the recipe is saved. You get a web page
in a nicer font, and then you are on your own: pinching to zoom with a knuckle,
losing your place in a wall of text, waking the screen with the back of your
hand because the front of it has raw chicken on it.

Cook mode is Saucier's answer to the twenty minutes that actually matter.

## One step at a time, in type you can read

Tap **Make It** and the recipe stops being a document. You get the step you are
on, large enough to read from across the counter, with the ingredients that step
needs right there rather than scrolled away at the top of the page.

**Next** is the big obvious control. **Previous** is there but small, because of
the two it is the one you want by accident far less often. The screen stays
awake throughout.

## Timers that behave like kitchen timers

Start a timer from a step and three things happen that a web page cannot do:

- It **keeps running in the background**, so switching apps or locking the phone
  does not quietly cancel your proving time.
- It appears on your **Lock Screen as a Live Activity**, so the time left is
  visible without unlocking anything.
- It **notifies you** when it is done, and tapping that notification puts you
  back on the exact recipe and step it came from.

Several can run at once. Two recipes can run at once. The **On Now** list is the
one place that shows you everything you currently have going, and it is
reachable from every screen in the app — because what is on the hob is a
property of your kitchen, not of whatever screen you happen to be looking at.

## Ask out loud

Switch Voice on and you can stop touching the phone altogether:

- *"Read me the next step."*
- *"How much butter?"*
- *"What do I still need?"*
- *"Set a timer for twelve minutes."*
- *"I've no buttermilk."*

Saucier listens, answers out loud, and acts — moving the step on, starting the
timer, scaling the amounts. It runs on your device, so it works with no signal
and sends nothing anywhere. [More about Ask Saucier →]({{ '/ai-recipe-assistant/' | relative_url }})

## Ticking things off

Ingredients tick off as you use them, so coming back after a distraction does
not mean re-reading the list to work out whether the butter already went in.
The tick list is shared with the assistant's answers — tick it in one place and
it is ticked in the other.

## The grocery list, before you start

Before any of the above, there is the shopping. Send one ingredient or the whole
recipe to your **Reminders grocery list**, and the button changes to confirm it
landed. It used to look identical whether you had tapped it or not, which meant
the only way to be sure was to open Reminders — and the usual response to that
is to tap everything twice.

If you have planned several meals, the [Week tab]({{ '/meal-planning/' | relative_url }})
also builds one in-app grocery list from all of them.

## Accessibility is not an afterthought

Cook mode is tested at the largest Dynamic Type sizes, where the controls stack
rather than truncate, and every icon-only control carries a VoiceOver label.
Reduce Motion is honoured. A recipe you cannot read at the size you need it is
not a recipe you can cook.
